How to Approach People

You're not a salesperson reading from a script. You're someone who genuinely helps businesses solve real problems. Here's the approach that works:

1. Be a customer first

Visit the business. Buy their food, get a quote from the tradie, attend their event. Start as a real person, not a sales rep. You'll understand their business better and they'll be more open to talking.

2. Ask, don't pitch

Start with a genuine question: "Do you take online orders?" or "What do you use for job bookings?" Listen to their answer. If they have a pain point, you'll hear it. If they don't, move on — not every business needs an app.

3. Show, don't tell

If they're interested, open the Products tab and tap "Show Client". A 60-second visual demo is worth more than 10 minutes of explaining. Let them ask questions — don't oversell.

4. Make it easy to say yes (and easy to say no)

Send a quote using the Quote Builder. Always say "no stress if it's not the right time." Removing pressure makes people more likely to engage, not less. If they need to think, book a specific follow-up date.

5. Follow up with value, not pressure

Every follow-up should add something new — a demo video, a case study, an observation about their business. Never send "just checking in." If they don't reply after 3 touches, send the breakup message and move on.

6. Log everything

Add every conversation to My Leads — even ones that said no. Track the stage, add notes. When they say yes, mark as Won and let the team handle the build. You just earned your commission.

How to Introduce Yourself

THE RULE: Know three things, say them naturally. Never memorise a script word-for-word.
1. Who you help (food trucks, tradies, event organisers)
2. One specific result for a real business (not features, not pricing)
3. A question that opens a conversation (never end with a pitch)
Casual intro

"I work with a lot of local food trucks and tradies — I help them get their own app for things like online ordering or job bookings. One of the food trucks I work with reckons it saved them about 4 hours a week just on phone orders. What sort of setup are you using at the moment?"

When someone asks what you do

"So basically, I help small businesses get their own app — ordering, bookings, that kind of thing. Fully branded to them, their customers never see our name. One sparky I work with replaced three separate tools he was paying $200 a month for with one app. It depends on the business though — what do you do?"

Longer conversation

Don't pitch — show. Open the Products tab, tap "Show Client" on the relevant app type. Walk them through the slides and let them ask questions. The presentation does the heavy lifting.

Tip: Be a customer first. Buy their food, use their service. Start as a genuine person, not a rep.

Conversation Starters

The 80/20 rule: spend 80% of the conversation listening, 20% talking. Ask about their business, their frustrations, their goals. The more they talk, the more you understand what they actually need.

Getting them talking

"How did you get into [their business]?"People love telling their origin story. It builds genuine rapport and they'll relax because you're not pitching yet.
"What's the busiest part of your week look like?"This naturally reveals pain points without you having to dig. They'll tell you where they're struggling.
"What do you use right now for [orders/bookings/jobs]?"Non-threatening, just curious. Most will say phone calls, texts, or pen and paper — which is your opening.
"Have you tried anything before that didn't work out?"Shows you're not naive. Most have tried Shopify, Wix, or an expensive agency. Their bad experience is your opportunity.
"If you could fix one thing about running the business, what would it be?"Gets to the real problem. Whatever they say is what you sell against — not features, not pricing.

When they're ready

Let them close themselvesIf they ask "so how does it work?" or "what does it cost?" — they're interested. Answer simply. Don't oversell.
Summarise what they said back to them"So you're doing about 50 orders on Saturday mornings all by phone, and you reckon you lose a few because people can't get through — is that right?" Then: "Yeah, that's exactly what the app fixes."
Make the next step easy"Want me to send you a quick demo of what it'd look like for [Business Name]? Takes 60 seconds to watch." Not a 30-minute call — just a demo link.
After they say yesMark the lead as Won in My Leads, note the agreed price, and let the boss know. That's it — the team handles everything from here.

When They Push Back

Objections aren't rejection — they're questions in disguise. The person is still talking to you, which means they're interested. Don't get defensive. Just answer honestly.
"It's too expensive."
Don't argue. Ask: "Compared to what?" If they mean DIY: "Fair enough — Shopify is $79+/month and you still have to build it yourself and figure out the tech. This is done for you." If they mean generally: "What would it cost you to keep doing it by hand for another year?" Let them do the maths.
"I need to think about it."
Don't push. Say: "Yeah of course. What's the main thing you want to mull over — the cost side or how it'd actually work day-to-day?" Address that one thing, then book a follow-up: "Can I check back in on Thursday?" Always leave with a specific date.
"We already have a website."
"That's cool — this isn't a website replacement. Your website tells people about you. This lets customers actually do something — order, book, pay. They work alongside each other."
"I'm not techy."
"Honestly, most of the people I work with aren't either. That's kind of the whole point — we build it, we manage it, you just use it. If you can use Facebook, you can use the dashboard. And if you get stuck, you message me."
"Can't I just use Shopify / Wix?"
"You could, yeah. Shopify's about $79/month before any add-ons, and you'd need to build it yourself which takes a few weeks. The main difference is this is built specifically for your business and you don't have to touch the tech side at all. Up to you though — happy to show you what the difference looks like."
"What happens if you go out of business?"
"Reasonable question. The app runs on Cloudflare — they're one of the biggest infrastructure companies in the world. If we disappeared tomorrow, the app keeps running as-is. We can also hand over the code if that's a genuine concern for you."
"I need to talk to my partner / accountant."
"No worries at all. Want me to send through a one-pager they can look at? And when works for me to check back in — end of the week?" Don't leave without a date.
"I've been burned before."
"Honestly, that's really common. The reason our stuff is different is we don't start from scratch each time — we have proven templates we customise. So there's no scope creep, no mystery invoices, and it's done in days, not months. And there's no lock-in — if it's not right, you just cancel."

FAQ — Questions Prospects Ask

"How does the payment processing work?"We integrate with Stripe, PayPal, or Square — whichever the client prefers. Payments go directly to the client's account. We never touch their money. Stripe/PayPal take a standard transaction fee (about 1.75-2.9% + 30c).
"Can I update the content myself?"Yes — every app comes with a dashboard where you can update menus, products, pricing, blog posts, etc. If you ever get stuck, just message us and we'll do it for you.
"What if I want a feature you don't have?"We can build custom features. The base packages cover 90% of what businesses need, but if there's something specific, we'll quote it separately. Usually pretty affordable because our platform handles the hard stuff.

"How many customers can use it at once?"Unlimited. It runs on Cloudflare's global network — same infrastructure that powers some of the largest websites in the world. It can handle thousands of simultaneous users without slowing down.
